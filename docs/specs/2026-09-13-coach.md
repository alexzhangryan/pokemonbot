# M9: the coach

Design, 2026-09-13, Claude Code. Status: being built; Alex chose M9 over the
two search-input candidates `docs/STATUS.md` left open after M8.

`docs/06-coach-and-evaluation.md` part I specifies the coach and
`docs/07-observability.md` section 2 specifies its output: a stream of
`analysis` events keyed to the turns of the trace they annotate, so that a
reviewed game is the original trace plus an overlay rather than a separate
artifact. `docs/01-plan.md` says the coach "is not a separate system. It is
the same core run offline with a larger budget." This spec turns those three
documents into data structures, a rule for every label and tag, fixed before
any game is reviewed, and an order of work.

## 1. What the coach is, in this codebase

Every decision the agent makes is a matrix game: rows are our joint actions,
columns are the opponent's, cells are `TurnModel.value` (one turn resolved
analytically, then `win_prob`), and the answer is `solve_both`. The coach
re-solves each decision of a finished game offline with the pruning removed
and the information state made explicit, then compares what was played
against the equilibrium in the two ways section 1 of `docs/06` names:

- **Ex-ante loss.** $L_{\text{ante}}(a) = V(\sigma^*) - V(a, \sigma^*_{\text{opp}})$:
  the game value minus the played row's value against the opponent's
  equilibrium column mix. `matrix.best_response_value(A, y)` is that second
  term. Zero for any row in the support, by the equilibrium's indifference
  property, so a non-zero number always means "off the support".
- **Ex-post loss.** $L_{\text{post}}(a) = \max_{a'} A_{\text{post}}[a', j^*] - A_{\text{post}}[a, j^*]$,
  where $j^*$ is the column the opponent actually played and
  $A_{\text{post}}$ is the matrix recomputed under full information.

Both are win-probability differences, which is what makes them commensurable
with the eval bar and with each other, and why the evaluation's `calibrated`
flag travels on every analysis event (section 6).

Two sources of games, one analysis:

| source | the position | our rows | their columns | what was played |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a trace of the agent's own game | `turn_start.state` | the unpruned `candidates.joint` (the request's legal set, up to 300) | rebuilt offline (section 3) | `equilibrium.chosen_action`; theirs from the next `turn_result` |
| a Showdown replay of anyone's game | `replay_state.Observer.view(side)` | the product of `policy_data`'s per-slot choice sets (D65: only what the acting player knew) | rebuilt offline | `policy_data`'s matched choice per slot, both sides |

The second source is why the coach can review a human's game and not only
the agent's, and it reuses M7's reconstruction rather than adding a third
path from a log to a board.

## 2. The information model, stated so the loss means something

"Given what was knowable" is a choice about columns and about the opponent's
hidden stats, and it differs between the two sources.

| | ex-ante columns and hypothesis | ex-post columns and hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| trace, ordinary | revealed moves only, pessimistic constant stats (`opponent_candidates` with no `believed_moves`; `OpponentHypothesis()`), the incumbent's own model | the same, unless `--opponent-team` supplies the registered sets (a mirror match's team file), in which case a `TruthOracle` over `truth_from_team_file` fills both seams |
| replay, open sheets (the Bo3 ladder) | the sheet: the human saw it, so it was knowable. `TruthOracle` over `truth_from_replay` for the opposing side | the same sheet |
| replay, closed sheets | revealed moves only | the same |

The analysis event records which of these produced its numbers
(`information.ante`, `information.post`), so no reader mistakes a
revealed-only loss for a full-information one. The belief filter is not a
source here: `data/priors/` needs the corpus, which is not on this machine
(`docs/STATUS.md`), and the coach must run on a fresh clone. The seam is
`SetSource`; a belief-backed source drops in without touching the analysis.

Our own side's items and abilities are known to the player on either source,
so the ex-ante model uses `BeliefEffects` (which reads them off the snapshot)
rather than the incumbent's `NoEffects`. This is a deliberate, small
departure from the live agent and is recorded on the event as
`model: "analytic-one-turn"`, `effects: "known-own-side"`.

The played row is always in the row set: the request's legal set contains it
by construction on a trace, and on a replay the reconstruction matched it.
The played column is appended to the column set when the offline column
builder did not produce it (a switch, or a move the revealed-only model had
not seen), because $L_{\text{post}}$ needs it.

## 3. The offline budget

No clock. Rows are the whole legal set rather than `k = 10`: a replay turn
is about 120 joint actions, a trace turn up to 300. Columns come from
`opponent_candidates` at a budget of 25, which keeps all sixteen joints of two
known four-move sets, plus the played column. That is at most about 5,000
`TurnModel` cells a turn, under a second on this machine, so nothing here is
sampled and nothing needs common random numbers beyond what the analytic
model already provides by construction.

The two-ply model and the simulator payoff are not used. M8 measured neither
apart from the one-turn model (D71, D72), and `docs/08` names the risk of the
coach growing its own evaluation. The seam is `CellModel`; a `--model twoply`
flag is a one-line addition if a later gate justifies it.

## 4. The rule, fixed now

All thresholds are in win-probability points and are hand-set. `docs/06`
section 2 says they should be calibrated against rating bands; that needs the
corpus and its ratings, which this machine does not have, so the calibration
is a named follow-up (section 9) and the constants live in one place
(`champions/coach/classify.py`) with this sentence beside them.

**Support.** A row is on the support when its equilibrium weight exceeds
`matrix.MASS_THRESHOLD` or its ex-ante loss is within $10^{-6}$ of zero. The
second clause matters: the LP returns one equilibrium of possibly many, and a
row it happened not to weight can still be exactly as good.

**Classification**, from $L_{\text{ante}}$:

| label | condition |
| --- | --- |
| Best | on the support, and no row carries more equilibrium weight |
| Solid | on the support, not Best |
| Inaccuracy | off the support, $L_{\text{ante}} < 0.05$ |
| Mistake | off the support, $0.05 \le L_{\text{ante}} < 0.15$ |
| Blunder | off the support, $L_{\text{ante}} \ge 0.15$ |

**Tags**, each independent of the others and of the label:

| tag | condition |
| --- | --- |
| Forced | the equilibrium is pure (`Equilibrium.is_pure`) and every other row's ex-ante value is at least 0.05 below the game value |
| Read | the played row is on the support, and the opponent's played column is a minimiser of that row (within $10^{-6}$) that costs at least 0.03 against the row's value under the equilibrium column mix |
| Gamble | the played row carried under 0.10 equilibrium weight, and its ex-post cell exceeds the game value by at least 0.03 |
| Unlucky | the played row is on the support or an Inaccuracy, and the realised position is at least 0.10 below the played cell's expected value |
| Lucky | the realised position is at least 0.10 above the played cell's expected value |

`Lucky` is not in `docs/06`'s list. It is the mirror of `Unlucky` and is
included because a player who wins a turn on a roll should see that as
clearly as one who loses it; the four named tags are implemented exactly as
specified.

"Realised position" is `win_prob` of the next turn's snapshot (or the final
snapshot after the last turn). "Expected value of the played cell" is
$A_{\text{post}}[a, j^*]$. Their difference, `luck`, is reported signed on
every event whether or not it crosses the threshold. It is not purely rolls:
everything the one-turn model does not represent (items, abilities,
secondary effects, a switch-in the model scores as a lost turn) lands in the
same number. The roll decomposition is reported beside it as the model's
own branches, `TurnModel.outcomes` with each branch's probability, value and
faints, so a reader can see how much of the gap the rolls alone explain.

**Critical turns.** Two lists, both in the game summary: the three largest
$L_{\text{ante}}$ (avoidable), and the three largest drops in the win
probability curve between consecutive turns (what happened). `docs/06`
section 4 calls the first the better approximation; both are cheap and they
disagree in exactly the games where the distinction is the point.

**The curve.** `evaluate(turn_start.state).win_prob` per turn, from the
acting side, plus the final state. On a trace this recomputes the number the
agent recorded; the two are asserted equal in the tests, since a coach that
reads a different evaluation from the one that played is `docs/08`'s named
failure.

**The preview pseudo-turn.** `docs/06` section 4 wants the bring-4 verdict
from the exact $15 \times 15$ solve. There is nothing to solve it with: the
preview value model was rejected out of sample (D39) and the separability
test that would license a self-play matrix was never run (D56), so the agent
itself brings four uniformly at random and its `preview_decision` names the
missing fields as `pending`. The coach's preview event does the same: the
bring, the leads, the opponent's observed bring, and `pending` naming the
verdict with D39 and D56 as the reason, rather than inventing a number.

## 5. Components

### 5.1 `champions/coach/truth.py`: a `SetSource` from a truth table

`TruthOracle(truths: Mapping[str, TruthSet], dex)` answers `stats_for`,
`set_for` and `believed_moves` the way `champions.agents.oracle.TeamOracle`
does, over any `dict[str, TruthSet]` rather than a team file, so the
open-sheet half of a replay (`belief.evaluate.truth_from_replay`) and a team
export (`truth_from_team_file`) are the same object to the analysis.
`stats_for` returns None without points (an open sheet never carries them,
D33) and `BeliefHypothesis` then falls back to the pessimistic constant, which
is the documented behaviour of that seam.

### 5.2 `champions/coach/decisions.py`: one shape from two sources

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Decision:
    turn: int
    snapshot: dict            # the acting side's view at turn start
    rows: list[dict]          # our legal joint actions, described shape
    played: int | None        # index into rows; None if the log did not say
    their_played: dict | None # the opponent's realised joint action, column shape
    next_snapshot: dict       # the view at the next turn start, or the final one
    for_seq: int              # the seq of the base-trace event this annotates
    recorded: dict            # what the live agent wrote: game_value, chosen, watchdog
    belief: dict | None       # the belief event's summary, when the trace has one

@dataclass(frozen=True)
class Game:
    battle_id, format_id, side, player, opponent, result, turns
    events: list[dict]        # the base trace, original or synthesised
    decisions: list[Decision]
    ante_truths / post_truths: dict[str, TruthSet] | None
    source: "trace" | "replay"
```

`from_trace(events, dex, opponent_team=None)` reads the events the tracer
already emits. The opponent's realised action for turn $t$ is the first
`move` or voluntary `switch` observation per opposing slot with `turn == t`,
found in the `turn_result` emitted at $t + 1$ or in `battle_end`'s
`final_observations`; a slot with none (flinched, asleep, empty) is a
`none` slot, which is what actually happened to it.

`from_replay(log, battle_id, side, dex)` synthesises the base trace a live
agent would have written (`battle_start`, `preview_decision`, per turn a
`turn_start` with the evaluation, a `turn_result` with the parser's
observations of the previous turn, and an `equilibrium` with
`strategy: "human"`) and builds each decision from `policy_data`'s per-slot
`Decision`s for both sides, joined into joint actions the way the live
enumeration joins them (minus same-target double switches). A turn where the
acting side's choice is unrecorded for a slot has `played = None` and is
reported with its equilibrium and no loss. `side` may be `p1`, `p2` or a
player name.

### 5.3 `champions/coach/analyze.py`: the numbers

`analyze_decision(decision, ante: Models, post: Models) -> TurnAnalysis` and
`analyze_game(game, ...) -> GameAnalysis`. `Models` bundles a `TurnModel`, a
`believed_moves` callable and a name for the information state. Per
decision: build columns, append the played column if absent, solve, compute
$L_{\text{ante}}$, $L_{\text{post}}$, `luck`, the roll branches, the label
and tags of section 4. Per game: the curve, the summary counts, the critical
turns, the preview pseudo-turn.

### 5.4 `champions/coach/classify.py`: the rule as code

Thresholds and the two pure functions `classify` and `tags`, unit tested on
synthetic matrices for every branch of section 4, so the rule cannot drift
between this document and the run.

### 5.5 `champions/coach/explain.py`: the writeup

A deterministic template from the numbers: what was played, the label and
tags, the equilibrium mix as percentages, what the opponent did, the best
reply after the fact, and where the win probability went. With `--llm`, the
template's facts are handed to `search.llm.client_from_env` (D68's mocked
client, cached) with an instruction to rewrite them as coaching prose without
adding a number the facts do not contain; any `LLMError` falls back to the
template. The critical turns get a writeup; every turn gets the template's
one-line summary. `docs/06` section 3 puts the language model in the coach
unconditionally; the template is what makes the coach work on a fresh clone
with no model reachable, and the tests exercise the template only.

### 5.6 `champions/coach/report.py`: the overlay and the document

`overlay(game, analysis) -> list[dict]`: the base events with an `analysis`
event inserted after each turn's `equilibrium` (`scope: "turn"`), one after
`preview_decision` (`scope: "preview"`) and one before `battle_end`
(`scope: "game"`), re-sequenced contiguously with each `analysis` carrying
`for_seq` and `turn`. The result passes `trace.validate.validate_events`,
which is the contract the viewer reads.

`markdown(game, analysis) -> str`: the review as a document. A header with
the result, ex-ante loss per turn and in total, the label counts and the
critical turns; a table with one row per turn (win probability, played,
label, tags, both losses); the writeups.

### 5.7 `scripts/review.py` (`make review`)

```
python scripts/review.py traces/foo.jsonl                # a trace
python scripts/review.py replay.log --side p1            # a saved replay log
python scripts/review.py gen9championsvgc2026regmb-123   # fetched from the replay site
python scripts/review.py <url> --side alice --opponent-team data/teams/regmb-worlds.txt
```

Writes `<stem>.review.jsonl` beside the input (or `--out`) and
`<stem>.review.md`, and prints the markdown. `--llm` for the writeup, `--k`
for the column budget.

## 6. What the coach does when the evaluation is uncalibrated

`docs/STATUS.md` left this to whoever wrote the coach. Decided: it runs, and
says so. Every `analysis` event carries `calibrated`, the report header
prints one line when it is False, and no number is suppressed. The ordering
of rows is unaffected by a monotone miscalibration, so the label is still
informative, and the losses are still the numbers the agent played by. A
coach that refused to run on a fresh clone would never be tried. Recorded as
D76.

## 8. Testing

TDD, as M7 and M8.

- **Classification and tags.** Synthetic matrices: a pure equilibrium with a
  dominated alternative is Forced; an off-support row at each loss band gets
  the band's label; an on-support row against its minimising column is Read;
  a low-weight row whose cell beats the value is Gamble; a realised value
  0.10 under the cell is Unlucky. Loss is never negative beyond solver
  tolerance; every support row has zero loss.
- **Trace source.** A hand-built trace of two turns yields two decisions with
  the right rows, played index and opponent column; the played column is
  appended when absent; a watchdog fallback is carried through `recorded`.
  On a real gate trace, the recomputed curve equals the recorded evaluation
  turn for turn.
- **Replay source.** On `test_policy_data.LOG`: turn 1 for `p1` has 120 rows,
  the played joint is Iron Head at Starmie plus Tailwind, the opponent's
  column is Ice Beam at Corviknight plus a switch to Incineroar; `p2`'s view
  is the mirror; the closed-sheet log falls back to revealed moves. The
  synthesised trace validates.
- **Overlay.** Validates, is contiguous, keeps every original event in order,
  and each `analysis` names the event it annotates.
- **Report.** Renders without a model, states `calibrated`, lists the
  critical turns.

## 9. What this will not do

- **No calibrated thresholds.** Section 4's constants are hand-set until the
  corpus and its ratings are on a machine that runs this; the calibration
  and `docs/06` section 8's predictive-validity check (ex-ante loss against
  rating) are one script over scraped replays and are the first follow-up.
- **No bring-4 verdict.** Section 4 says why; the pseudo-turn is emitted
  with the reason.
- **No belief-backed information state.** The seam exists; the prior does
  not on this machine.
- **No review client.** M10 renders the overlay; the markdown is the
  interim reader.
- **No Champions ingestion.** `docs/01` asks whether the coach should accept
  manually entered Champions games. It accepts any protocol log, so a
  Champions game transcribed into protocol form is reviewable today; a
  transcription tool is a separate deliverable, and the question stays open
  in `docs/STATUS.md`.
- **No two-ply or simulator payoff.** Section 3.
